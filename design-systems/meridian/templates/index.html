The central finding

Better AI workflows do not remove the human from the loop. They make it easier for the human to know where to look, what to question, and when to act.

02

Trust is a workflow, not a feature.

Generating a first draft is no longer the hard part. The work has moved downstream: checking claims, finding the original evidence, and deciding whether the answer is good enough to use.

In our illustrative scenario, teams that introduced an explicit review rubric improved steadily. The difference was not a more capable model. It was a more legible handoff between the model and the person.1

A useful distinction

Accuracy asks whether an answer is correct.

Verifiability asks how easily someone can establish that it is correct.

03

Three things that matter

  1. Make uncertainty visible.

    A missing source is information. Show it beside the claim, rather than burying a general disclaimer at the bottom of the document.

  2. Put evidence within reach.

    Review is faster when the claim, its source, and the relevant excerpt live together. Do not ask a reader to reconstruct the reasoning across five tabs.

  3. Measure what survives review.

    Raw output volume rewards the wrong behavior. Track accepted work, corrections, and the time it takes to reach a defensible decision.

The practical test

Could a colleague who was not in the meeting understand both the conclusion and its limits in two minutes?

The useful unit is not the generated answer. It is the decision someone can confidently make with it.

Meridian · Working principle 04
04

Start with one consequential handoff.

Choose a recurring document that people already spend time checking. Define what “ready to use” means in three or four observable criteria. Then compare a small batch of documents before and after the change.

Keep the experiment narrow. The goal is not to redesign every process. It is to learn which part of the handoff creates the most avoidable work.

Recommended next step

Run a two-week review pilot. Assign an owner, keep the model constant, and measure review time alongside the rate of accepted outputs. Record exceptions, not just averages.

How to adapt this report to your own evidence

Replace the scenario with a real cohort and define the rubric before collecting results. Disclose sample size, sampling method, exclusions, and uncertainty. A before-and-after comparison alone does not isolate the effect of the review process.

Notes & methodology

  1. Illustrative scenario. All organizations, measures, and results on this page are fictional. The numbers are included to demonstrate charts, hierarchy, annotations, and data tables.
  2. Metric definitions. Review time: median minutes per output. Pass rate: share of outputs meeting every criterion in a predetermined rubric. Changes in pass rate are percentage points, not relative percentages.
  3. Use in practice. Add primary-source links, observation dates, limitations, and review ownership before distributing a real report.
